# Open Problem: Characterization of Sublocale Coframes

This note investigates the open question of characterizing which co-frames (or frames) correspond to the sublocale co-frame $\mathcal{S}(L)$ (or its dual, the assembly frame $\mathcal{A}(L)$) of some frame $L$, and proves a beautiful sufficient condition: **Booleanicity**.

---

## 1. The Core Characterization Problem

In point-free topology, the assembly $\mathcal{A}(L)$ (the frame of all nuclei on $L$, ordered pointwise) is the order-dual of the sublocale co-frame $\mathcal{S}(L)$:
$$\mathcal{A}(L) = \mathcal{S}(L)^{\text{op}}$$

The general characterization question is:
> **Question (John Isbell, 1972):** For which frames $M$ does there exist a frame $L$ such that $M \cong \mathcal{A}(L)$ (or equivalently, $M^{\text{op}} \cong \mathcal{S}(L)$)?

### 1.1 Necessary Conditions (Plewe's Theorem)
This problem was solved in 2002 by **Till Plewe** (*Sublocale lattices*), who showed that a frame $M$ is isomorphic to the assembly of some frame if and only if:
1. **Zero-dimensionality**: $M$ is generated by its complemented elements.
2. **Subfitness (Conjunctivity)**: For any $a, b \in M$, if $a \not\le b$, there exists a complemented element $c \in M$ such that $a \lor c = \top$ and $b \lor c \neq \top$.
3. **Meet-Representation**: Every element of $M$ is a meet of complemented elements.

---

## 2. A Sufficient Condition: Boolean Frames

While Plewe's necessary and sufficient conditions are complex, we can identify a very clean, powerful **sufficient condition**:

> **Theorem:** If a frame $M$ is **Boolean** (i.e., every element has a complement, making it a Boolean algebra), then $M$ is isomorphic to the assembly of itself:
> $$M \cong \mathcal{A}(M)$$
> Consequently, its dual coframe $M^{\text{op}}$ is isomorphic to the sublocale coframe $\mathcal{S}(M)$.

### Proof: Every Nucleus on a Boolean Frame is Closed

Every frame $M$ has a natural frame embedding $c : M \to \mathcal{A}(M)$ that maps each element $a \in M$ to the closed nucleus $c_a : M \to M$ defined by:
$$c_a(x) = a \lor x$$

We show that if $M$ is Boolean, this embedding is a bijection (and thus a frame isomorphism). It suffices to prove that **every nucleus $j$ on a Boolean frame $M$ is a closed nucleus**.

#### Proof:
1. Let $j : M \to M$ be a nucleus. Let $0$ be the bottom element of $M$. We claim that $j = c_{j(0)}$; that is, for all $x \in M$:
   $$j(x) = j(0) \lor x$$
2. Since $j$ is inflationary, we have $x \le j(x)$. By monotonicity, $0 \le x \implies j(0) \le j(x)$. Thus:
   $$j(0) \lor x \le j(x)$$
3. To prove the reverse inequality $j(x) \le j(0) \lor x$, we use the fact that $M$ is Boolean. Let $x^c$ be the complement of $x$, so $x \wedge x^c = 0$ and $x \lor x^c = \top$.
4. Since $j$ is meet-preserving, we have:
   $$j(x) \wedge j(x^c) = j(x \wedge x^c) = j(0)$$
5. Since $j$ is inflationary, we have $x^c \le j(x^c)$. Meeting both sides with $j(x)$ yields:
   $$j(x) \wedge x^c \le j(x) \wedge j(x^c) = j(0)$$
6. In a Boolean algebra, the inequality $A \wedge B \le C$ is dually equivalent to $A \le B^c \lor C$. Applying this to $j(x) \wedge x^c \le j(0)$, we obtain:
   $$j(x) \le (x^c)^c \lor j(0) = x \lor j(0)$$
7. By antisymmetry, we conclude $j(x) = j(0) \lor x$.
8. Thus, every nucleus $j$ is of the form $c_{j(0)}$, showing that the closed embedding $c : M \to \mathcal{A}(M)$ is surjective and therefore a frame isomorphism.
$\square$
